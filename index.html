Introduction

Workforce sustainability depends on balancing operational demand with staff capacity and wellbeing. High workloads, frequent overtime, and low satisfaction may signal pressure that deserves further review. This executive report uses interactive Tableau dashboard outputs to present patterns that can inform workforce-planning discussions.

The report intentionally keeps the two data sources separate. The staff scheduling data describe departmental workload and wellbeing indicators. The employee attrition data are synthetic and are used only to illustrate workforce-risk and retention analysis techniques.

Methods

Data preparation and analysis

  • Staff scheduling data were analysed by department using estimated weekly hours, patient load, overtime hours, and satisfaction score.
  • A 40-hour line was used as an illustrative weekly planning benchmark; it is not a clinical staffing-standard threshold.
  • High-workload counts were summarised by department to identify the relative concentration of staff at or above the selected workload threshold.
  • The separate synthetic workforce dataset was analysed using employee count, attrition, overtime status, work-life balance, job role, and project count.
  • Results are descriptive. They identify patterns and associations, not causal effects.

Visualisation approach

Tableau dashboards were created to compare departments, display the distribution of estimated weekly hours, explore patient-load and overtime relationships, and summarise synthetic attrition patterns. Department filtering supports focused operational review in the staff scheduling dashboard.

Results

Departmental weekly hours

Average estimated weekly hours differ across ER, General Medicine, ICU, and Pediatrics. Review departments above the 40-hour planning benchmark alongside workload distribution and staffing context.

High-workload staff

High-workload staff counts can help prioritise departments for closer review, rota adjustment, or workload-redistribution discussions.

Patient-load relationship

Patient load and estimated weekly hours should be considered jointly. A higher workload pattern alone does not establish that patient load caused longer hours.

Overtime and satisfaction

Overtime and satisfaction score provide useful wellbeing indicators, but they should be interpreted with contextual qualitative evidence from staff and managers.

Synthetic Workforce-Risk and Retention

Figure 2. Synthetic Workforce-Risk and Retention Dashboard. This analysis uses synthetic employee records and is illustrative only.

The synthetic dashboard contains 10,000 employee records and reports an overall attrition rate of 14.0%, an overtime rate of 29%, and a low work-life-balance rate of 25.0%. Attrition is higher in the overtime group than in the non-overtime group, and lower work-life-balance ratings are associated with higher attrition. These are descriptive synthetic-data patterns, not real organisational results or proof of causation.

Conclusion and Recommendations

The dashboards indicate the value of monitoring workforce risk through a combined view of workload, demand, overtime, and wellbeing rather than relying on a single metric.

  1. Review departments with high estimated weekly hours and large high-workload staff counts, beginning with local rota and capacity discussions.
  2. Monitor patient load alongside staffing levels, shift design, case complexity, and non-clinical workload before making resource decisions.
  3. Review overtime patterns regularly and consider targeted support, workload redistribution, or scheduling changes where prolonged overtime is identified.
  4. Combine dashboard evidence with staff feedback, absence patterns, turnover data, and management judgement before acting.
  5. Use the synthetic retention dashboard only as a demonstration of analytical method; repeat the analysis with validated local data before applying it to real workforce policy.

Data Sources and Limitations

Data sources

  • Staff Scheduling Dataset: illustrative departmental records containing estimated weekly hours, patient load, overtime hours, satisfaction score, and department.
  • employee_attrition_performance.csv: a synthetic dataset containing 10,000 employee records used for illustrative workforce-risk and retention analysis.

Limitations

  • Both analyses are descriptive and cannot demonstrate causal relationships.
  • The 40-hour line is an illustrative planning benchmark, not a clinical staffing adequacy standard or a regulatory threshold.
  • Estimated weekly hours, patient load, overtime, and satisfaction may not capture case complexity, skill mix, leave, vacancies, shift patterns, or local operational conditions.
  • The attrition dataset is synthetic; it must not be interpreted as evidence about a real organisation or employee population.
  • The two datasets were not joined because they represent separate contexts and do not share a valid employee-level linkage.
